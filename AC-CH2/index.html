Chapter 2: The Village of Many

Part 1: Welcome to the Red Sector

The trees finally thinned. Golden light spilled through the canopy in fractured shafts, painting the path ahead in warm stripes. The wet crashing and gurgling roars of the Maksur had faded somewhere far behind them—swallowed by the dense undergrowth or simply losing interest. For the first time since the fields, the air felt almost still.

Strawberry slowed to a skip. Her yellow optics brightened.

STRAWBERRY
“[Happy expression] We’re here! Welcome to the Red Sector!”

The path opened into a wide clearing ringed by low wooden houses and patched agro-domes. Everything looked… functional. Crops grew in neat rows. Tools leaned against walls. A few worker drones moved about their daily tasks under the soft glow of hanging lantern-fruit.

And then the Strawberries appeared.

One stepped out from behind a drying rack, pink hair slightly less mossy than the first. Another emerged from a doorway, sweater cleaner but still dotted with tiny fungal caps. A third waved from a rooftop. Within seconds, half a dozen identical figures had gathered, all smiling with the same soft, round yellow optics.

MULTIPLE STRAWBERRIES
“Welcome, our new visitors...Please, let us take your bags and stuff!”

They spoke in near-perfect unison, voices overlapping like a slightly delayed echo. Hands reached out—gentle, insistent—for the scanner, the cutter, the half-empty satchels. Lex clutched his equipment tighter. Vex simply stared.

LEX
“Captain… aren’t you wondering why there are multiple of her?”

Fai barely glanced up. She was already crouched over the scanner, jabbing at its cracked display with increasing irritation. Blue error lines flickered across the screen. The distress-signal tracker had flatlined completely sometime during the run through the woods.

FAI
“Really, you all look the same to me, Vex.”

She didn’t even look at Lex when she said it. Her focus stayed locked on the broken device.

LEX
“…I’m Lex.”

A soft metallic giggle sounded directly behind his left shoulder.

A Strawberry that hadn’t been there a second ago tilted her head, yellow optics inches from his face. She spoke in the same syrupy, crackling tone as all the others.

STRAWBERRY
“There’s only one… just multiple bodies. [Giggles] Must be wonderful having free will…”
LEX
“What do you mean…?”

The Strawberry behind him only smiled wider and stepped around to join the others.

STRAWBERRY
“It means your rooms are ready. Feel free to greet your neighborhoods~”

She...they...began guiding the trio deeper into the village without waiting for further questions.

The walk was quiet in a way that felt deliberate.

Some worker drones that looking nothing like Strawberry, others uncomfortably just waved as they passed. One pair of drones farming side by side raised their hands in greeting at the exact same moment, same angle, same soft smile. Another dropped a basket of glowing fruit; a third, several meters away, flinched downward at the identical second even though she couldn’t have seen it. No one asked who the visitors were. No one stared for longer than a polite second. They simply continued farming, sweeping, hanging laundry, smiling.

Fai’s boots scuffed against the packed dirt. She still hadn’t looked up from the scanner.

FAI
“Damn it, this thing’s busted… I don’t understand. The signal was there. It was there.”

Vex stepped closer. Her metallic hand settled on Fai’s shoulder very gently, lightly and steady, the closest thing to comfort the depressed drone ever offered. Her optics visor stayed dimmed, but the gesture was clear.

FAI
“…You’re right, Vex. I should relax.”

She didn’t put the scanner away, but she stopped jabbing at it.

They reached a short row of small cabins near the edge of the village. Each door had a name roughly etched into the wood: FAI. LEX. VEX. The lettering looked freshly carved, the wood still pale where the bark had been scraped away.

One of the Strawberries gestured grandly.

STRAWBERRY
“[Smile] Here’s your rooms. Each door has your name on it, just… huh…”

She paused. Her head tilted sharply to the side, as if listening to something none of the others could hear. The cheerful expression flickered for less than a second. Her optics dimming, her smile tightening then snapped back into place.

STRAWBERRY
“Oh… my boss needs me. Have fun… Bye~”

She turned and skipped away toward the larger central building without another word. The remaining Strawberries drifted off in different directions, returning to their tasks as though the visitors had never arrived.

Fai stared after the retreating figure, yellow-streaked hair falling across her face.

FAI
“…Boss?”

Behind them, the village continued their work. The lantern-fruit swayed gently in a wind that carried no scent of Maksur.

But this is was far from over for them...

The night settled over the Red Sector like a thick, damp blanket. The lantern-fruit dimmed to a soft amber glow. The smiling Villager drones had long since returned to their cabins or simply stood motionless in the fields, optics dark, as if waiting for morning instructions.

Inside the cabin marked LEX, Lex sat in his bed, his head tilted and optics offline. His charging port humming quietly. Across the short path, stood Vex’s in her slumber in the corner of her own room, one arm dangling.

Fai’s cabin stayed dark.

She sat on the edge of the narrow cot, labcoat still on, yellow-streaked hair falling into her face. Sleep refused to come. Every time she closed her eyes the skeleton in the fields flashed behind her lids—tattered lab coat, single yellow stripe, picked clean. The broken scanner lay on the floor where she had thrown it earlier. No signal. No mentor. No results. No progress. Nothing.

She stood.

The door creaked only once as she slipped outside. No Strawberries waited on the path. The village felt emptier under the dark, yet somehow more watchful. Fai stayed on guard just in case, her boots silent on the packed dirt, and moved toward the larger central building Strawberry had hurried to earlier.

The side entrance was unlocked.









Inside, rows of old computer terminals lined the walls—bulky, pre-collapse models coated in a thin layer of dust and moss. Some screens were cracked. Others still held faint standby lights. Power hummed somewhere deeper in the walls. The village had electricity. Real electricity. Not scrap-powered jury-rigs.

Fai’s sharp grin returned.

FAI
“There we go…”

She wiped a screen clean with her sleeve and powered the nearest terminal. The machine coughed static, then flickered to life in grainy green text. Files. Logs. Fragmented data. She dug through folders with the same manic focus she once used in the asteroid labs—searching for anything tied to the distress signal, the mentor’s encryption signature, anything.

Nothing clean came up.

Instead, older files surfaced. Human records. Planetary status reports. One folder labeled simply EARTH.

She opened it.









Images of wars and ruins. Then something worse.

Absolute Solver. Disassembly Drones. The systematic erasure of a world that had once been home to billions. The files were incomplete, corrupted, but the story was clear enough. Earth was gone. Humanity’s cradle had been hollowed out by the very things JCJenson had built.

Fai stared at the screen for a long moment.

She tried to picture her mentor’s face while reading the reports—the man who had taught her to laugh while the universe tried to eat her. The memory slipped. Edges blurred. His voice, his eyes, the exact shape of the scar he always claimed was from a “lab accident”… Her mind simply would not hold the full picture.

FAI
“…Master used to talk about Earth...”

She scrolled further, jaw tightening.

FAI
“They left us on that rock for years. Forgot the colony the second the numbers stopped looking good. Cults. Purges. Power cells. Prayer protocols. We tore each other apart while they played god with the rest of the universe.”

She felt nothing close to sorrow for the destroyed homeworld. Only a cold, familiar bitterness.

Then the screen flickered again.

A new image loaded—low quality, surveillance footage from somewhere on this very planet. Yellow optics. Bright. Round. The exact same shade that glowed behind every Strawberry’s visor.

Fai leaned closer.

The footage showed more of them. Dozens. Spreading through the fields, the forests, the abandoned agro-domes. Same face. Same soft smile. Same yellow eyes. Moving in quiet, coordinated patterns that no single drone should be capable of.

FAI
“Hold on… wasn’t there more of her…?”

Her voice dropped to a whisper.

FAI
“Spreading like a pestilence.”

She reached for another terminal, already half-lost in the data, fingers flying across keys that still responded. Clues about the signal. Clues about the boss. Clues about whatever the yellow eyes really meant.

Somewhere behind her, a soft metallic giggle drifted through the open doorway.

Fai did not turn around immediately. Her attention had already fractured—half on the screens, half on the growing realization that the village was not simply strange.

It was definley hiding something or someone.

And Fai had always had trouble focusing on the things that did not matter to her mission.

Fai reached for the mouse without taking her eyes off the monitor. She missed it by an inch, and her palm came down on a plump pink mushroom growing beside the keyboard.

Its cap squished beneath her hand with a soft, wet pop.

The air crackled with spores...

...But the spores had only just begun to spread.
